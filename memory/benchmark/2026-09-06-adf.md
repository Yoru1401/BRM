# The renderer, before and after baking

GTX 1650, 1280x720, release. Method rules are in
[history/wrong-turns](../history/wrong-turns.md#measurement) and they are not
optional.

## The two numbers

Taken on 2026-09-06, an hour apart, on the same machine. **They are not a
before/after of the same scene** — the analytic renderer could not draw a torus
and the baked one cannot draw twenty-four brushes. Read them as what each
renderer costs to fill a similar fraction of a 720p screen.

| renderer | scene | lights | median | min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analytic, one box brush plus nine blends | `showcase`, 24 shapes | 1, no shadows | 10.19 ms | 8.89 |
| **baked ADF** | torus, 11380 bricks | 1, no shadows | **2.62 ms** | 2.20 |
| **baked ADF** | torus, 11380 bricks | **3, one shadow caster** | **2.64 ms** | 2.15 |
| **baked ADF** | **20 m map, 9202 bricks, 40 objects** | **3, one shadow caster** | **2.62 ms** | 2.15 |
| **baked ADF** | **316 m world, 37831 bricks, 0.49 m voxels** | 3, one shadow caster | **3.06 ms** | 2.26 |
| **baked ADF** | **1037 m world, 101348 bricks, 1.10 m voxels** | 4, shadowed sun | **3.40 ms** | 2.21 |

Three runs each, spread under 0.15 ms, so these are stable rather than lucky.

**The 1 km row is the one to quote.** The torus covered about an eighth of the
screen, so its number could not be told apart from the empty-scene floor. The
20 m map fills the frame and reads the same 2.62 ms. The open world fills it
with a kilometre of terrain, 220 structures and four arches under a shadowed
sun, and costs **0.8 ms more than the empty floor**.

Between 20 m and 1 km the frame time moves by less than a millisecond while the
brick count goes up 11x. **Cost tracks screen coverage and step count, not world
size** — which is the property a baked field was chosen for.

### One run in the scaling sweep was vsync-capped and is not reported

`--size 1000 --bricks 65536` (1.65 m voxels) read a median of **16.674 and
16.677 ms** across two runs. That is 1000/60 to three decimals. `PresentMode` was
`Immediate` and the driver capped anyway. The bench prints a warning for exactly
this and it was filtered out of the captured output. **Discarded, not re-run** —
the finer bake is both better looking and honestly measured.

**The third row is the one that matters.** Adding two lights and a shadow caster
cost 0.02 ms. Under the analytic field a second evaluator inlined into the shadow
march cost the whole frame whether or not it ran, which is why the shadow proxy
existed at all — see [reference/lights](../reference/lights.md). A texture fetch
is not a second evaluator, so the proxy was deleted and shadows now read the real
field, holes included.

**2.62 ms is also the empty-scene floor** measured on 2026-09-03 (Bevy, present
and the CPU, resolution-independent). The marching is not free, it is smaller
than the noise on this floor at this coverage. Do not quote it as zero, and
**re-measure at a coverage where the field fills the screen** before believing
the field is cheap in general.

## What the analytic renderer measured, for the record

The four benchmark files that carried these were deleted with the code they
described. What is worth keeping:

- **0.306 ms per shape per frame, dead linear** before any acceleration. That
  linearity is what the uniform grid had to beat, and it is the reason a baked
  field wins: cost stops depending on how many brushes authored the scene.
- **The grid was worth 78%** on the sparse scene (`spread:80`, 65.6 to 14.45)
  and 65% on the dense one. Deleted anyway on 2026-09-06 — a page table over
  bricks is the same idea with the shape loop removed.
- **Removing the subdivided quad mesh beat removing the cone march**: 15.08 with
  the pass off but the mesh still there, 11.05 with both gone.
- **Sparse costs 2.4x dense at the same shape count.** Step count was the second
  cost axis, and sparse is the workload a game actually has.
- **The rebuild cost 0.8 ms of CPU per frame, of which ~0.75 was moving
  zeroes** — fixed-size grid buffers padded and copied whatever the scene held.
  Baking deleted the whole system: statics are uploaded once.

## Resolution, and the knob that is still there

Measured 2026-09-03/06 against the analytic marcher. The *shape* of these
survives baking — marching is still roughly linear in pixels — but the
magnitudes do not, so re-measure before quoting them.

- An empty field costs the same at any resolution: **1.95 ms**, Bevy and the CPU.
- 2.25x the pixels cost **2.4x the marching**, slightly superlinear, because
  `ray_march` ties its hit threshold to pixel height.
- **`--render-scale` was the largest single lever**: 18.7 ms to 11.1 at 1080p,
  about 40%.
- **`--detail 1.5` is worth about 3%, not the 21% first claimed**, and it moves
  silhouettes by 1.5-3.1% of pixels. `DETAIL` stays 1.0.

## The bake itself

CPU, single call at startup, `ComputeTaskPool` across cores.

| model | triangles | voxel | bricks | bake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| torus, 48 rings | 2304 | 15.6 mm | 11936 | 0.97 s |
| torus, 96 rings | 9216 | 15.6 mm | 11380 | 2.4 s |
| torus, 192 rings | 36864 | 15.6 mm | 10996 | 5.8 s |
| the 20 m map | 12516 | 78.1 mm | 9202 | 3.9 s |
| the world at 316 m | 105346 | 494 mm | 37831 | 5.4 s |
| **the world at 1037 m** | 105346 | 1099 mm | 101348 | **8.5 s** |

The map is slower per triangle than the torus because its triangles are large:
a floor slab spans many bricks, so it lands in many per-brick lists. Cost is
roughly linear in *binned* triangles, not in mesh triangles, and flat in brick
count, because the cost is
point-triangle tests against a per-brick list. **Nothing streams and nothing is
incremental**: the whole model is baked before the first frame is drawn.

Two optimisations landed with the open world and are already in these numbers:

- **A triangle AABB reject before the closest-point test.** `signed_distance`
  skips a triangle whose box is further than the best distance so far. It is the
  difference between a 1 km world baking in 8 s and not baking at all.
- **The per-brick bin was narrowed from one brick size to `RANGE_VOXELS`.** A
  brick is allocated only if a triangle comes within the *encoded band*, not
  within a whole brick. Roughly halves the brick count and shortens every list;
  the empty-space step gives back the difference through the clearance term.

## 2026-09-06 evening: dynamics landed, and the clock stopped being readable

Folding dynamic capsules into the field, turning over-relaxation off and gating
contact on banded samples all landed together. **Their combined cost was not
measured, because the bench became unusable.**

What happened, in order, all on the same binary and scene:

| block | median | min | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 7.997 - 7.878 | 2.836 - 6.611 | plausible |
| base, later | 6.653 | 5.081 | plausible |
| `--omega 1.2` | 16.678 | 14.776 | **vsync** |
| `--bodies 0` | 3.983 | 2.901 | plausible |
| base, immediately after | 16.660 | 14.999 | **vsync** |

**Two A blocks of the same configuration read 6.65 and 16.66.** The bench's own
60 Hz warning fired on half the runs. Per
[history/wrong-turns](../history/wrong-turns.md#measurement) that is an artefact,
and the whole comparison is discarded rather than reported.

The one thing the numbers weakly suggest — `--bodies 0` at 3.98 against a base of
6.65 — is that **the dynamic fold costs real time**, which is what the 14 ms
lesson in [reference/lights](../reference/lights.md) would predict of anything
added to the innermost loop. A bounding-sphere reject over all dynamics was added
for it and is **also unmeasured**.

**Re-taken 2026-09-07 on a quiet machine, and it reads clean:**

| scene | median | min | spread over 3 runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 km world, 4 lights, shadowed sun, dynamics, materials | **4.13 ms** | 3.59 | 0.05 ms |

Against 3.40 ms before dynamics landed, so **the whole of dynamic capsules,
per-brick materials and a specular term costs about 0.7 ms**, 21%.

**2026-09-07, materials moved to voxel resolution:** 5.05 ms on the same scene
(4.4-4.8 before the buried-brick pass, which removed 20% of the bricks and did
not move the frame). The `paint` volume adds 50 MB and about **0.4 ms**, all of it in the one
fetch per shaded pixel — the march never reads it. Generating the dynamic shape
functions from Rust instead of writing them in WGSL cost nothing measurable: the
`switch` over two primitives is as cheap as the inlined capsule was.

The 6.65 ms reading the night before was real but has been largely recovered.
What did it: `surface_normal` no longer folds. It decides once whether the hit
belongs to a capsule or to the bake, then takes **either** the capsule's exact
analytic gradient (no taps at all) **or** four taps of `baked_probe` — instead of
four taps of both fields. That is four fewer dynamic-loop evaluations per shaded
pixel, and it is also what makes the union crisp.

### The band was widened and put back

`RANGE_VOXELS` 4 to 8 was tried: the step heatmap got visibly cooler, the brick
count went 101348 to 123544, the atlas 99 to 119 MB and the bake 8.5 to 15.0 s.
Frame time appeared to get much worse, but on the same unreadable clock. Reverted
to 4 on the grounds that the bake cost is certain and the frame win is not.

## Where a single volume runs out

| limit | value | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| bake time | ~12 s per 100k bricks | the practical cap, and the only one hit so far |
| `--bricks` | 150000 default | the retry loop coarsens the voxel until the count fits |
| atlas texture | 2048 texels per axis | 204³ slots, about 8.5M bricks, 8.5 GB |
| `PAGE_LIMIT` | 8M words, 32 MB | the page grid is dense, so it caps brick *grid* extent at ~200³ |
| `MARGIN` | 2 bricks | the volume is the model's AABB, so a stray object still enlarges everything |

The page table being dense is the first structural wall: a 4 km world at 1 m
voxels wants 500³ bricks, which is 125M words. That is what the clipmap in
[plan/open-world](../plan/open-world.md) exists to solve.
